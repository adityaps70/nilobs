Mooring & access: what SIRE 2.0 inspectors ask and find

Mooring observations often come from lines, tails and winch brakes against the mooring system management plan, and from gangway and pilot ladder rigging.

Do this before the inspector arrives: Check mooring line certificates against the MSMP and the brake test record.

What the inspector wants to see

  • Mooring system management plan up to date
  • Line and tail certificates, MBL checked against the ship design MBL
  • Winch brake test records
  • Gangway and pilot ladder rigging, with permits where required

Questions inspectors ask about Mooring & access

Is mooring familiarisation only for first-time joiners?
No. Give it to all mooring crew, especially where equipment differs from the usual set-up (e.g. hydraulic brakes, mooring zone markings).
What is the maximum weight of a heaving line monkey fist?
Not more than 500 g (COSWP section 26.3.6).
Is there a procedure for using a double (criss-cross) rope stopper?
One is expected; its absence was flagged.
What record is needed before mooring winch brake testing?
A record that the hydraulic jack was calibrated before the test.
On which layer is the brake test done when the drum is not split?
On the outermost (top) layer.
At what load should the winch brake render, and what goes into the calculation?
60% of SDMBL. Inputs: jack pressure (P), piston area (A), lever from drum centre to jack point (L), drum diameter (Dd, not winch) and rope diameter (Dr).

Real Mooring & access observations, and how to avoid them

From 59 de-identified observations in SIRE 2.0, CDI and pre-vetting reports from 2025 and 2026. Wording paraphrased; ship, crew and company names removed.

A rubber step near the bottom of the port pilot ladder in use was deformed; the ladder was replaced during the inspection.
Avoid it: Inspect every step of the pilot ladder before rigging and replace ladders with damaged steps.
SIRE 2.0 · Chapter 5 Safety management · Hardware observation
Interviewee said the deformed pilot ladder step had been missed during the safety inspection through oversight.
Avoid it: Inspect pilot ladders step by step, not just a glance at the rolled-up ladder.
SIRE 2.0 · Chapter 5 Safety management · Human observation
A locking nut on the brake tightening screw was being used as a stopper to set the mooring winch brake torque.
Avoid it: Set winch brakes as per the brake test results and maker's guidance, and know how the setting is marked.
SIRE 2.0 · Chapter 9 Mooring & anchoring · Hardware observation
The accompanying deck officer was not fully familiar with how mooring winch brake rendering indications are set.
Avoid it: Know your winch brake rendering marks, how they were set after brake testing and how to use them.
SIRE 2.0 · Chapter 9 Mooring & anchoring · Human observation
Also recorded as a learning gap: the deck officer could not fully explain the setting of winch brake rendering marks.
Avoid it: Include winch brake setting and rendering marks in mooring familiarisation and toolbox talks.
SIRE 2.0 · Chapter 9 Mooring & anchoring · Human observation
Photo not representative: the mooring winch brake rendering marks and arrow indicators were found ineffective on board.
Avoid it: Keep winch brake marks clearly painted and make sure uploaded photos reflect current condition.
SIRE 2.0 · Chapter 11 General appearance & condition
Interviewee did not understand the mooring winch brakes and could not say why brakes are tightened only to the rendering point setting.
Avoid it: Understand why brakes are set to render at a set load: the line should slip before it parts.
SIRE 2.0 · Chapter 9 Mooring & anchoring · Human observation
The stud in the first link of the port anchor chain had come loose and shifted against the next link.
Avoid it: Inspect anchor chain links and studs during anchor inspections and when heaving up.
